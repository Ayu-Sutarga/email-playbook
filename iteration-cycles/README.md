# Iteration Cycles: Rewriting One Sequence Three Times

A cold sequence is never finished on the first draft. This is how I rewrote one 3-email sequence (introducing a keynote speaker to US speaker bureaus and event producers) across **three cycles**, and the thinking behind each change.

---

## Cycle 1: The warm introduction
**Assumption:** "They just haven't heard of this speaker yet."

**Approach**
- Email 1: *"Have you heard about {{EXPERT_NAME}}?"* + reach numbers + famous stages + video
- Email 2: a clickable reel thumbnail, *"see them on stage instead of me talking about them"* + ask for 30 minutes
- Email 3: excellence scores + a client quote + ask again

**What I learned:** leading with *who the speaker is* makes the email about us, not about the reader. Name recognition alone doesn't create urgency.

---

## Cycle 2: Problem-first
**New assumption:** "They still don't know the speaker, so lead with a problem they already have."

**Changes**
- Subject lines became **problems**: *"The 'humanity gap' in your AI roster?"*, *"Moving past information overload at your next event"*
- Each email opened with a **market shift** the reader recognises (AI fatigue, attrition, summit burnout)
- The speaker became **the solution** to that problem, not the headline
- Added a **framework** (a named method) to make the offer feel concrete
- Asks became more specific: *"10 minutes next Tuesday or Wednesday?"*

**Why:** people pay attention to their own problems first. A named framework signals substance.

---

## Cycle 3: Refresh with new programming
**New assumption:** "Same audience, but the offer has evolved, so the language should too."

**Changes**
- Updated to new keynote titles and programme names
- Softer, more consultative opening (*"a different lens on your {{YEAR}} leadership agenda"*)
- Added a **resource** (brochure) so there's value even without a meeting
- Positioned the topic as a **"hard skill"** grounded in evidence, not a nice-to-have

---

## 📌 The pattern
| | Cycle 1 | Cycle 2 | Cycle 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opens with | The speaker | The reader's problem | The reader's agenda |
| Proof | Fame, reach | Data + framework | Evidence + resource |
| Ask | 30-min call | Specific 10-min slot | Chat + a resource to keep |

## 🔁 How to run your own cycles
1. Write down your **assumption** about the reader before each cycle.
2. Change **one big thing** per cycle (angle, ask or proof), so you know what made the difference.
3. Read the **replies**, including "not now" and "not a fit". They tell you what to change next.
4. Keep spintax (`{{RANDOM|...}}`) and personalisation consistent across cycles to protect deliverability.
