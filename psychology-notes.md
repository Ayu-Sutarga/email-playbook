# Psychology Notes: Why These Emails Work

Each technique here is used somewhere in this playbook. I'm NLP-certified, and I draw on persuasion research and behavioural economics. These are the principles I come back to most.

---

### 1. Lead with their world, not yours
People filter information by relevance to themselves. An opening about *their* problem gets read; an opening about *your* expert gets skimmed.
**Used in:** cold Email 1, iteration Cycle 2

### 2. Pattern interrupt: the stat hook
A surprising number breaks autopilot reading. *"Did you know…"* creates a small gap the reader wants to close.
**Used in:** the monthly theme system
**Caution:** the number must be real and sourced. One false stat ruins trust.

### 3. Reframing
Changing the frame changes the value. *"Empathy isn't a soft skill. It's a performance driver"* moves a topic from "nice to have" to "business priority". This comes straight from NLP reframing.
**Used in:** cold Email 1, Cycle 3

### 4. Social proof
We trust what people like us already trust. One specific client quote outperforms ten adjectives.
**Used in:** cold Email 2

### 5. The choice between two yeses
*"Would you prefer the outline, or a quick chat?"* Both answers move things forward, and choosing feels easier than deciding yes or no.
**Used in:** cold Email 2

### 6. Lowering the ask (door-in-the-face, reversed in time)
After a bigger ask, a smaller one feels reasonable by comparison. And a small yes builds commitment for bigger ones later.
**Used in:** cold Email 3 (full keynote → short session)

### 7. Concreteness through named frameworks
A named method (an acronym or a titled programme) feels more real and repeatable than "my approach". It also gives partners something easy to repeat to their clients.
**Used in:** iteration Cycle 2

### 8. Show, don't tell
*"See them on stage instead of me talking about them."* A video or reel transfers emotion that text can't.
**Used in:** iteration Cycle 1, the video thumbnail block

### 9. Specific time asks
*"10 minutes next Tuesday or Wednesday?"* is easier to say yes to than *"Can we connect sometime?"* Specific asks reduce thinking effort.
**Used in:** iteration Cycle 2

### 10. Leave the door open
A warm final line (*"I'm here whenever you'd like to continue"*) protects the relationship. "Not now" often becomes "yes" six months later.
**Used in:** cold Email 3
