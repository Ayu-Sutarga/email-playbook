# Case Study: Why the "Make Everything Clickable" Newsletter Failed

## The setup
I built a quarterly newsletter for a keynote speaker's audience using **light HTML**: a headline, short update text, one hero image, **one main button**, contact details and social links. It was written directly in the email tool's editor so it looked close to a personal email.

## The change
Leadership wanted **every news item to be clickable**, with each highlight linked separately. Building that properly would have meant a more complex template, and I didn't have time to learn and build it alongside my other responsibilities. So a freelancer was hired to build a heavier, link-rich version.

## What happened
- **Open rates dropped**
- **Most emails landed in spam**
- The freelancer cost was **high** for the result
- The new format was **discontinued**

## Why it likely happened
Spam filters look at signals that separate personal email from mass marketing. A heavy template typically sends more of the wrong signals:
- **Many links** in one email look promotional
- **More code and images** relative to text make it look like a designed blast
- **Low engagement** in the first sends teaches inbox providers to filter future ones too, so the problem compounds

## The lesson
> More clickable doesn't mean more clicks. If the email doesn't reach the inbox, nothing in it gets clicked.

For newsletters sent from an outreach tool:
1. Keep **one main call to action**, and link to a landing page that holds all the news
2. Keep the **text-to-image balance** text-heavy
3. Test new formats on a **small segment first**, and compare open rates before rolling out
4. Count the **full cost**: build cost + lost reach

## What I'd do differently
Propose a **small A/B test** before switching: send the light version to half the list and the clickable version to the other half, then let the open rates decide.
